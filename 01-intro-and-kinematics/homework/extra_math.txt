<summary>Curvature derivation (via circle-fitting).</summary>
Match the curve and a circle through the same point with the same tangent; requiring that they coincide up to the second-order term gives the radius of curvature.

Let $\vec{r}(t)$ be the path. With $v = 1$, arc length equals time and $|\dot{\vec{r}}| = 1$.
**1. Curve — Taylor in time $\delta\tau$:**
$$
\vec{r}(\tau + \delta\tau) = \vec{r}(\tau) + \delta\tau \, \dot{\vec{r}}(\tau) + \frac{\delta\tau^2}{2} \ddot{\vec{r}}(\tau) + O(\delta\tau^3).
$$

**2. Circle —** Take the circle in the plane of $\dot{\vec{r}}$ and $\ddot{\vec{r}}$ through $\vec{r}(\tau)$ with tangent $\dot{\vec{r}}(\tau)$, radius $R$, center in the direction of $\ddot{\vec{r}}$. Parametrise by arc length $s$; at $s = 0$ we are at $\vec{r}(\tau)$ with $\vec{T} = \dot{\vec{r}}$, and unit normal toward the center $\vec{N} = \ddot{\vec{r}}/|\ddot{\vec{r}}|$. The circle is $\vec{r}_c(s) = \vec{r}(\tau) + R(1 - \cos(s/R))\vec{N} + R\sin(s/R)\,\vec{T}$, so $\vec{r}_c'(0) = \vec{T}$ and $\vec{r}_c''(0) = \vec{N}/R = \kappa\vec{N}$ with $\kappa = 1/R$. In time (since $\mathrm{d}s/\mathrm{d}t = 1$):
$$
\vec{r}_c(\tau + \delta\tau) = \vec{r}(\tau) + \delta\tau \, \dot{\vec{r}}(\tau) + \frac{\delta\tau^2}{2} \kappa\vec{N} + O(\delta\tau^3).
$$

**3. Match to second order:** $\ddot{\vec{r}}(\tau) = \kappa\vec{N}$ $\Rightarrow$ $|\ddot{\vec{r}}| = \kappa$, so $R = 1/\kappa$.

**4. From the EOM,** $\ddot{\vec{r}} = (-u_1\sin\theta - u_2\cos\theta\sin\phi,\; u_1\cos\theta - u_2\sin\theta\sin\phi,\; u_2\cos\phi)$, hence $|\ddot{\vec{r}}|^2 = u_1^2 + u_2^2$ and $\kappa = \sqrt{u_1^2 + u_2^2}$.
